Research

Drawing on cognitive and developmental science, I identify abilities that humans develop early in life but AI models still struggle with, and try to understand the computational mechanisms inside models that underlie these shortcomings. My long-term goal is to build models that learn and generalize more like humans. Representative papers are highlighted.

SMORE: Minimal-Pair Videos for Social, Moral and Rational Reasoning in MLLMs
Ajeya Mandikal, A. Garg, V. D. Jain, D. Khandelwal, Meenakshi Khosla, Vineeth N. Balasubramanian
NeurIPS Workshop on Developmental Perspectives on AI (DevAI), 2026  under review at ICLR 2027
OpenReview

A benchmark of 427 contrastive minimal video pairs that isolate the socially relevant variable while holding everything else fixed. Under Joint-Pair Accuracy, which requires both videos of a pair to be judged correctly, Gemini 3.1 Pro scores 63% against 96% for humans, yet layer-wise probes show the relevant social information is still decodable inside models that answer wrongly.

Seeing Through a Slit: Anorthoscopic Perception in Multimodal Large Language Models
Ajeya Mandikal, Vineeth N. Balasubramanian
NeurIPS Workshop on Developmental Perspectives on AI (DevAI), 2026
OpenReview

Words move behind a narrow slit, so only a sliver is ever visible. Humans read every word at every slit width; MLLMs that are near-perfect on fully visible stimuli collapse to 3% or below at a 5% slit, exposing a gap in integrating visual information over time.
