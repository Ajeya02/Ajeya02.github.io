Research

SMORE: Minimal-Pair Videos for Social, Moral and Rational Reasoning in MLLMs
Ajeya Mandikal, Aditya Garg, Jain Vardhman Divakar, Dikshant Khandelwal, Meenakshi Khosla, Vineeth N. Balasubramanian
NeurIPS Workshop on Developmental Perspectives on AI (DevAI), 2026  under review at ICLR 2027
OpenReview

SMORE is a benchmark of 427 contrastive minimal video pairs spanning rational, social, and moral judgment. Each pair isolates the socially relevant variable while holding all other visual and contextual factors fixed, so model errors can be attributed to social reasoning rather than visual confounds.

We introduce Joint-Pair Accuracy, which requires both videos in a pair to be judged correctly. It reveals a 33-point gap between Gemini 3.1 Pro (63%) and humans (96%). Layer-wise linear probing across five open-source MLLMs shows that social reasoning remains decodable even when models answer incorrectly.

Seeing Through a Slit: Anorthoscopic Perception in Multimodal Large Language Models
Ajeya Mandikal, Vineeth N. Balasubramanian
NeurIPS Workshop on Developmental Perspectives on AI (DevAI), 2026
OpenReview

Anorthoscopic perception is the early-developing human ability to recover a whole object from partial views revealed one after another through motion. We adapt the classical slit-viewing paradigm, in which a stimulus moves behind a narrow stationary slit so that only a small part is visible at any instant.

Our stimuli are 50 three-letter English words and 50 matched anagram pseudowords, seen through slits 20%, 10%, or 5% of the stimulus width. Humans identified every stimulus at every slit width. Models that are near-perfect on fully visible stimuli decline sharply as the slit narrows, and all six open-source MLLMs fall to 3% accuracy or below at the 5% slit, pointing to a gap in integrating visual information over time.

Benchmarking Deep Learning Models for Accurate and Efficient 2D Simulations of Microwave-Plasma Interaction
Ajeya Mandikal, K. Pandya, S. Gandha, B. Chaudhury
Physics of Plasmas, 33(5), 2026
paper

We develop deep-learning surrogates for 2D microwave–plasma simulations as computationally efficient alternatives to traditional FDTD solvers. We benchmark several image-to-image translation architectures, including a custom U-Net–ViT. The U-Net–ViT runs about 1000× faster than the FDTD solver with around 1% error.

Physics-Guided Fourier Neural Operator Framework for Accelerated Simulation of High-Power Microwave Breakdown
K. Pandya, P. Ghosh, Ajeya Mandikal, S. Gandha, B. Chaudhury
Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence, 167, 2026
paper

Simulating high-power microwave breakdown means solving Maxwell's equations coupled with a plasma continuity equation, which is slow. We replace the expensive FDTD field updates with a Fourier Neural Operator surrogate, coupled to a physics-based plasma solver. The hybrid framework reproduces streamer growth in near-perfect agreement with full FDTD-fluid simulations while running about 60× faster, bringing multi-day runs to under an hour.

Loss Function Sensitivity in Deep Learning Models for Investigation of Microwave-Plasma Interaction
S. Gandha, K. Pandya, Ajeya Mandikal, B. Chaudhury
IEEE Microwaves, Antennas, and Propagation Conference (MAPCON), 2025
paper

We study how the choice of loss function affects deep-learning models that predict microwave scattering from inhomogeneous plasma. We train U-Net and hybrid U-Net–ViT models with MSE, Huber, and a composite MSE–MAE–gradient-difference loss. The composite loss gives the best results on test samples, with higher SSIM and PSNR and lower MSE.
